**Microtime** (until now *tauri-punching-bag*) is a practice tool for drummers and musicians: a metronome and drum machine with programmable rhythms, a looper, and a live picture of what you play drawn against the beat, so you can see exactly where your notes land.

## Getting started

1. Download the `.dmg` below, open it and drag **Microtime** to Applications. It runs on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs, and it's signed and notarized, so it opens like any other app.
2. Allow microphone access when it asks.
3. Let the **setup wizard** run: it picks your devices, checks the microphone hears you, and measures your latency -- that measurement is what makes the picture line up with your playing.
4. **Use headphones** for the looper and the drums. On speakers the microphone hears the app as well as you.
5. Start with **Examples** in the settings panel. Each one teaches one idea and has a "try this" line.

Point at anything in the panel and the strip at the bottom says what it does. Shortcuts: ⌘P pause, ⌘L looper, ⌘R reroll random parameters, ⌘1–⌘0 recall parameter scenes, ⌘⌥1–⌘⌥0 open settings sections.

## New since 0.4.0

- **A new name: Microtime.** Your settings and presets carry over.
- **Eight built-in examples**: a metronome, 16ths against the grid, a basic backbeat, basic swing, a count-off into a groove, looping yourself, backbeat on/off (call and response with your own playing), and 3 against 4.
- **Parameter scenes**: save sets of parameter values and switch between them with ⌘1–⌘0 while you play. Settings sections moved to ⌘⌥1–⌘⌥0.
- **Change audio devices without restarting.** Microtime also follows macOS when the default device changes, and falls back cleanly when an interface is unplugged. Each input/output pair keeps its own latency.
- **The looper keeps running through the practice cycle**, and each section of the cycle has a **Record** switch -- untick it for a turn you want to hear back rather than record over. What the looper has recorded is cleared when the tempo or loop length changes, and on Restart.
- **Channels keep their identity**: switching to a device with fewer inputs no longer turns a pane's channel into the drums. An input the device doesn't have shows as "not connected" and comes back when the device does.
- A button in the top bar shows and hides the settings, and full screen can hide the top bar too (Layout → Hide top bar).
- Intel Macs are supported (universal build).
- If Microtime can't start its audio it now says why, instead of quitting silently, and it keeps a log you can send (Setup → Log → Reveal log file).

## Fixes

- A drum part whose first note isn't on beat 1 no longer plays a stray hit on beat 1 at launch, after Restart, or at each turn of the practice cycle; a drum with an offset no longer doubles its first hit at each turn.
- The looper no longer draws an echo you can't hear after the practice cycle wraps.
- Audio-thread fixes: several places that allocated memory while producing sound no longer do.
- Under the hood: moved to Tauri 2.
